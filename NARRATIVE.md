New ships begin a generated expedition: **rescue**, **knowledge recovery**, or **alien technology salvage**. The ship's name, catastrophe and surviving population come from its seed. Its existing profile supplies the context: pilgrims, passengers, prisoners or seed custodians. The journal shows the commission before you enter.

Use **G** at the loading bay's gold-marked console to accept the commission. Follow its lead, or explore another room to find evidence of recent activity. Bootprints, cable repairs, thawed tags, scrubbed specimen labels and ration shrines can reveal an alternative investigation. These discoveries happen when a player enters a ready room; streaming chunks or browsing a map does not discover them.

**I** opens the expedition journal. It separates the current goal, confirmed facts, field observations and unresolved leads. **L** cycles discovered records, **U** tracks the next unresolved lead on both maps, and **F4** switches between expedition and character pages. **Enter** closes the journal. Use **E/Q** on a cyan lift to reach another deck.

Investigate a refuge signal, witness archive or containment ledger with **G**. Clear exposed enemies near the site first. The evidence identifies the main target and a related optional operation. Newly revealed sites stay at their assigned coordinates; backtracking and save/load preserve discoveries and rewards. Completed operations establish checkpoints and award each participating player 75 salvage and two points.

At the main target, **G** opens a decision screen. **1, 2 or 3** confirms the displayed consequence; **Enter** leaves without deciding. The third option needs the optional operation. You must remain at the cleared target to commit a decision. A confirmed choice is final for that expedition.

| Motivation | Immediate commission | Preserve the refuge | Optional preparation and third outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rescue | Evacuate awake survivors; sleepers remain | Fortify the refuge; everyone stays aboard | Secure the evacuation relay to bring everyone home |
| Knowledge | Take the living star-chart archive; the refuge loses its navigator | Transmit a survey while leaving the archive aboard | Build a memory mirror to recover a complete copy |
| Salvage | Take the alien drive; the refuge loses power | Leave the drive powering life support | Repair the backup reactor, then take the drive safely |

Return to the bay and use the extraction beacon. It records a victory once, awards 250 salvage for the immediate commission, 180 for preserving the refuge, or 400 for the prepared outcome, in addition to the site's usual reward. The journal keeps the result. You can continue exploring afterward, or press **N**, then **Enter**, to begin another ship with the same character.

Gamepads open the journal with **left trigger + Back/Select**. In the expedition journal, **X/west** reads the next field record, **Y/north** tracks another lead, and **B/east** closes it. At a decision, **D-pad left/up/right** chooses options 1/2/3. The browser menu offers journal pages, field records, lead tracking and clearly labelled decision buttons for touch players. Opening a journal pauses the shared simulation in split-screen.

This first narrative milestone has **sixteen scene templates**: briefing; five environmental observations; three investigation sources; reactor, evacuation and memory-mirror operations; three motivation-specific targets; and extraction. The introductory arcs place their interactive destinations in the first four-by-four district across decks -1, 0 and +1, with preferences for suitable architecture and sectors. They use accessible service positions; environmental clues can be discovered farther away. The ship itself remains endless. Different exploration orders can reveal different investigation routes and assign later destinations from different anchors.

**Compatibility.** Save format 7 records narrative version 1, known sites, observations, completed operations, the tracked lead and final choice. Formats 3–6 keep their original ship and HARROW/ORISON campaign, including generator-3 architecture. Starting a new ship opts into the generated story while retaining character progression. Formats 1–2 continue to migrate profiles into a fresh ship. Old collectible ship records remain in the character's historical archive; expedition-specific facts live in the new journal.

The current optional operations change story outcomes, checkpoints and rewards. Existing incident trails remain part of world generation.
